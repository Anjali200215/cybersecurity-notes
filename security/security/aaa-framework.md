# 🔐 AAA Framework

## What is AAA?
AAA stands for:
- **Authentication**
- **Authorization**
- **Accounting**

It is a security framework that controls 
access to computer resources.

**Real life example:** Just like a office 
building — you show your ID card (Authentication), 
security checks what areas you can access 
(Authorization), and a register is maintained 
of your entry/exit (Accounting)!

## Authentication
Verifying WHO you are.

### Types of Authentication
- **Something you know** — Password, PIN
- **Something you have** — OTP, Smart card
- **Something you are** — Fingerprint, Face ID

### Multi Factor Authentication (MFA)
Using two or more authentication methods together.
Example: Password + OTP = MFA
This is much more secure!

## Authorization
Deciding WHAT you can access after authentication.

**Real life example:** In an office, all employees 
can enter the building (Authentication) but only 
managers can enter the server room (Authorization)!

### Types of Authorization
- **Role Based Access Control (RBAC)** — 
  Access based on your job role
- **Least Privilege** — Give only minimum 
  access needed for the job

## Accounting
Keeping track of WHAT you did on the system.

- Records login/logout times
- Tracks files accessed
- Monitors actions performed
- Used for auditing and investigation

**Real life example:** Just like a bank keeps 
record of every transaction — Accounting keeps 
record of every action on the network!

## AAA in Cybersecurity
AAA is very important for SOC Analysts because:
- Helps detect unauthorized access
- Provides logs for investigation
- Helps identify suspicious behavior
- Used in SIEM tools for monitoring
